# “Can you just restore last night's backup?”

<Panel title="The question">
  Asked in the worst hour of somebody's week:

  > We have the registry and the Compose files. Can you rebuild the host from
  > those and get the shops back?
</Panel>

Not from those two, no. They are the parts that can be regenerated, which is
exactly why they are the parts people have kept.

A restore needs the state that **cannot** be reconstructed from anything else.

/>

Generated Compose manifests are not on that list. They can be recreated, and
they will be.

## The registry is not a backup

This is the misunderstanding behind most of these tickets. The safe property
registry looks like a complete inventory of the fleet, and in a sense it is —
but it cannot recreate encoded property configuration by itself. Laravel's
deployment records are authoritative.

Backing up the registry and not the database gives you a list of shops you can
no longer build.

## Order matters

A restore is not one operation, and doing it out of order tends to produce a
host that looks up and serves the wrong thing.

Step two is the one that gets skipped. Restoring yesterday's state and then
installing today's binary is not a restore — it is an upgrade performed during
an incident, with no way to tell which of the two changes broke what.

## The honest answer about timing

We cannot tell you how long a restore takes without knowing the size of the
database and the number of properties. What we can tell you is that the last
step is not optional: a stack that reports healthy has not been verified until
real tenant data has been fetched through a real domain over a real certificate.
